## What the tool did

- Searched the literature for prior art on all three claims, in five independent
  sweeps, and recorded every query verbatim. The queries and the retrieval log
  are in `colophon/prior_art.py` and rendered to `results/prior_art.md`.
- Rendered that search into a PRISMA-S style appendix,
  `results/prisma_s_appendix.md` and `results/prisma_s_rows.csv`, generated by
  `colophon/prisma.py`. The appendix states the rule under which a negative
  prior-art claim is auditable, applies it, reports that the web-search arm of
  the search fails it, and lists every field the record cannot fill. The search
  itself was LLM assisted, which is why the appendix names the gaps a one-pass
  agentic search leaves unless it is instrumented to capture them.
- Read the prior spine-gsps harness and produced the reuse map and the list of
  failure modes that shaped this project's ledger schema and its guard rails.
- Wrote the Phase 0 code: `colophon/index.py`, `colophon/provenance.py`,
  `colophon/ledger.py`, `colophon/envinfo.py`, `colophon/paths.py`,
  `colophon/prior_art.py`, and the tests.
- Ran those modules and wrote the measurement write-ups in `results/`, which are
  generated from the data by the code rather than typed.
- Built the claims map, `colophon/claims_map.py` rendered to
  `results/claims_map.md` and `results/claims_map.csv`, which joins every claim
  id to its ledger row and to the artefact carrying it, and reports the joins
  that fail: orphan claims, uncited artefacts, broken derivations, retirement
  chains, pre-registration status, floor coverage and test coverage. It reports
  the pre-registered rows and does not edit them.

- Wrote and ran the Phase 3 execution of the PRE-06 sampling frame,
  `colophon/phase3.py` and `colophon/phase3_report.py`, which draws against the
  approved frame, fetches each series, validates it, records it and deletes it.
  The frame itself was written and pre-registered before this run and did not
  move when the execution fence came down: the strata, the seed, the registered
  minimum, the allocation rule and the byte gate are all as filed, and a test
  asserts each constant. The descent into `SegmentSequence` and the Algorithm
  Identification Macro is the one place where a wrong tag would have produced a
  confident null, so the tags are read from `colophon/standards.py` rather than
  retyped, and a test asserts the two agree and that the two tags the study brief
  gave wrongly cannot return.

- Assembled the claim 3 tabulation, `colophon/claim3.py` and
  `colophon/claim3_report.py` rendered to `CLAIM3.md`, over the census and the
  PRE-06 sample with nothing fetched. The classification of a provenance string
  is the part of this an LLM can get confidently wrong, so it is done by the
  project's own published, ordered rule table rather than by judgement, and
  every value the table has no rule for is printed verbatim in
  `results/claim3/t32_unclassified_values.csv` where it can be overruled. The
  first grading attempt used a permissive token test and returned 96 percent of
  objects as naming their producer, counting a viewer, a scanner vendor and an
  encoder sentinel as producer identity. It was caught by reading the deciding
  values rather than the summary, replaced with the rule table, and the
  corrected figure is 13.67 percent. The episode is recorded because the wrong
  number was plausible and would not have failed any test then in place.

- Supplied the **second adjudication pass** that PRE-03 registers,
  `colophon/adjudicate2.py`, and the pin deviation measurements in
  `colophon/deviations.py`. The second pass is by the same agent as the first
  and is named in Methods as an **intra-instrument repeatability check, not an
  independent human adjudication**: it shows the rule table is applied
  reproducibly and surfaces unstable classes, and it does not show either
  reading is correct. Blindness was partial, because `MORNING_REPORT.md`
  discloses several first-pass verdicts in prose, so 147 of 2,256 message
  classes are flagged pre-disclosed and the blind agreement is reported
  separately. Two regexes were corrected after the first comparison run; the
  verdicts they carry were not changed, and the departure is recorded rather
  than absorbed. A second human adjudicator remains outstanding.

- Resolved every citation in the manuscript. The drafted text carried author
  surnames with no years and no bibliography, which is the state in which a
  citation quietly stops pointing at anything, so each was resolved to a full
  record and the result was made a checked artefact rather than a typed list:
  `colophon/citations.py` reads the reference list and the bracketed markers and
  fails the build on a marker that resolves to nothing, an entry nothing cites,
  an entry with no DOI, arXiv id or URL, or a bare surname left in the text.
  **Resolving them changed four citations**, and each is a ledger row rather
  than a silent fix: the Gene Expression Omnibus study is Huang et al and not
  Ochoa et al, the software bill-of-materials study is the IEEE magazine and not
  the Symposium, the IHE AIR passage is in Rev 1.3 on printed page 17 and not
  Rev 1.2 on page 16, and CP-2273 is in the standard at edition 2026b rather
  than carrying a Final Text date that no source in this repository supports
  (REF-02 through REF-05). The AIR revision and page are re-checked against the
  pinned PDF offline on every test run, and the Correction Proposal statuses
  against the retrieved status-table rows on disk, so neither depends on the
  agent having read them correctly once.

- Assembled the submission package, `colophon/submission.py` rendered to
  `results/submission/`. Every file in it is a transformation of something
  already written: the manuscript documents in venue order, a title page, a
  blinded second copy, the tables, figure legends, the figures as EPS, a
  supplementary index and a generated checklist. The generator authors no
  sentence carrying a measurement except the cover letter, whose numbers are
  under the same ledger guard as the manuscript's. Two venue requirements were
  read rather than assumed and both changed the paper: the review model is
  double-blind, so there are two manuscript files, and the abstract limit is 250
  words against the repository abstract's 695, so a second abstract exists and
  what it drops is listed (SUB-02, SUB-03).

- Drafted the front matter and the WG-06 Correction Proposal. The proposal is
  drafted and not filed, and a test keeps that statement consistent across the
  abstract, the Discussion and `results/cp/`. The strongest fact in it was found
  by the Type re-verification rather than by the agent: Table C.8.20-5 already
  specifies the same attribute at Type 1C with the same condition, so the
  proposal copies wording the standard already contains (CPD-02). Its
  backwards-compatibility exposure is stated inside the proposal with the
  measured number rather than omitted (CPD-03).

- Read printed page 82 of the IHE AIR supplement and transcribed Table 6.5.3.1-1,
  rendered to `results/ihe_air_table.md` from the data in `colophon/standards.py`.
  A transcription is exactly the kind of output an LLM can produce confidently
  and wrongly, so it is controlled three ways: the source PDF is pinned by
  sha256 and rechecked whenever the write-up is regenerated; the table was read
  four ways that agree, including from the page's own ruling-line geometry by
  `scripts/pdf_table_geometry.py` and from a visual render, with the one
  extraction mode that disagrees recorded so it cannot be reintroduced; and the
  transcription is pinned cell by cell by a test.

## What the tool did not do

- It did not score any object's conformance. Conformance is scored only by
  `dciodvfy`, `dcmpschk`, `dicom-validator` and `highdicom`'s reader. No LLM
  output is used to decide whether an object conforms to the DICOM standard, and
  no LLM judgement enters any number in this repository.
- It did not choose the classification of a provenance string in a way a reader
  cannot check. The rules are an ordered table of substrings and regexes in
  `colophon/provenance.py`, and any single assignment can be verified by eye.
- It did not supply any figure from memory. Every number in `results/` is
  computed from the local IDC index at run time by code in this repository.
- It did not generate the DICOM data. IDC is the archive; this project reads it.

## How its output was controlled

Four controls, all of which are in the repository and all of which a reviewer can
run.

1. **Independent third-party validators.** The conformance arms of this study are
   scored by tools written by other people, none of them in this repository.
   This is the control that matters most, and it is why the never-adjudicate rule
   is stated before any code.
2. **The claims ledger.** `results/ledger.csv` carries every claim with the exact
   command that produced it, the source file, the floor it is measured against,
   and what the run dropped. `colophon.ledger.rates_without_floor` fails any rate
   quoted without a floor. Withdrawn claims stay with the reason, so a number
   that was wrong cannot quietly return. `results/claims_map.md` walks the
   ledger back to the artefacts and publishes the claims nothing carries and the
   artefacts no claim rests on, so a gap between the two is stated rather than
   left for a reviewer to find.
3. **Generated write-ups.** The markdown in `results/` is emitted by the same
   code that computes the numbers. A number cannot drift out of agreement with
   its table, because it is never typed.
4. **Tests.** `tests/` checks the ledger against the results files, checks that
   every claimed pin is in the lockfile, and checks house style. The prior
   project shipped two retired figures that survived a careful human read, which
   is the argument for testing prose rather than reading it.

## Errors found, and how each was actually found

Recorded because the value of a control is what it caught, not that it exists.
Each entry states the discovery route plainly. An earlier version of this section
was written in a way that implied the harness caught defects it did not catch,
which is the same failure mode as claiming coverage a study does not have.

**Found by eye, not by any test.**

- The acquisition-inheritance measurement matched series on absent identity, so
  a pair of nulls matched another pair of nulls. It scored the 40 Key Object
  Selection series as 100 percent inherited and GSPS as 52 percent. **No guard
  caught this. It was noticed by reading the output table and seeing a 100
  percent that could not be true.** Corrected to require both attributes
  present. The corrected figures are 9,971 and 10,491 series.
  Two guards were added afterwards, and they are the ones that would have caught
  it: a null-propagation assertion on every join key, and a rule that any rate
  whose denominator contains null join keys must declare the null count rather
  than silently dropping or silently matching those rows.
- The first provenance framing was not exhaustive. **Found by an independent
  recount of the same data, not by a test**, which is why exhaustiveness is now
  a property the taxonomy is tested for rather than a property it happened to
  have.
- The series-weighted rate is substantially one collection. **Found by external
  review challenging the unit of analysis**, not by anything in this repository.
  The leave-one-out sensitivity now runs on every rate.

- Figure 3's silent column was drawn from an analysis result whose every segment
  declares SegmentAlgorithmType MANUAL, so the Type 1C condition never fires in
  it and its silence is legitimate rather than the thing the paper is about.
  **Found by the author reading the figure and asking what made that column
  silent**, not by any test, and no test could have asked it. The check that
  followed is `colophon/silent_column.py` and it found four analysis results
  that are the truer case (FIG-02). It also surfaced a second thing nobody had
  asked about: two analysis results are recorded as naming a producer at no
  level while carrying a complete Algorithm Identification Macro, because the
  ordered rule table matches an institution or conversion rule first (FIG-03).
  Both are reported and neither is resolved.

- The pytest suite was first run under the wrong interpreter, the base conda
  environment rather than the project one, and reported 1 failure and 35 errors
  that do not exist. **Found by reading the traceback rather than the summary
  line**: every error was `ModuleNotFoundError: No module named 'idc_index'`,
  which is an environment fact and not a code fact. Under
  `envs/colophon/python.exe` the same suite passes. Recorded because a summary
  line is exactly the kind of output that gets quoted without its traceback.

**Found by a test.**
- The prose guard in `tests/test_prose.py` caught the consequences twice.
  It failed on `86.7` still sitting in the README after the ledger had moved to
  `86.44`, and it failed again on `378,153` being quoted in the README with no
  ledger row behind it. The second was a missing claim rather than a stale one,
  and the fix was to record the claim, `C3-11`, not to delete the number.
- The panel-availability check caught that `pydicom-seg` cannot be used at all:
  its current release imports a pydicom module removed in version 3, so
  including it would have meant downgrading below the pin the prior paper
  depends on. It is excluded with the reason recorded as ledger row `V-05`,
  rather than silently absent.
- Installing `pydicom-seg` downgraded numpy from 2.5.1 to 1.26.4, which would
  have broken JPEG-LS decoding for the 97 Segmentation series that need it. The
  downgrade was detected by testing imports immediately after installation and
  reverted.
- The blinded manuscript came out with two fewer references than the unblinded
  one, and therefore a different number against every entry after them, because
  the blinded declarations do not cite the self-citations the full ones do and
  the numbering was derived separately for each copy. **Caught by
  `tests/test_submission.py::test_the_two_versions_carry_the_same_reference_numbering`**,
  which exists because a reviewer's reference 12 has to be the editor's. The
  order is now computed once from the unblinded copy and used for both.
- Six defects reached a package that had already passed its own checklist, and
  **external review caught all six**, not the harness. Four were venue rules
  and two were internal contradictions: every figure carried a title drawn into
  the illustration, which the venue forbids; figure 1 set white on a medium grey
  at 1.98 to 1 against a required 4.5; Table 4's caption said a repository URL
  was present on all 1,001 highdicom-written objects when 1,001 is the
  Segmentation subset of 7,100 and the value is on 1,788 of them; figure 5's
  legend carried the retired reading of the Jaccard and its panel title
  contradicted its own figure title; the rule-ordering undercount at FIG-03 was
  in the ledger and in no section of the paper; and Table 3 printed a clipped
  SeriesDescription where a version belongs. **The checklist said yes to a
  figure set breaking three stated rules**, because the rows that covered them
  read `manual, not measured`. All six are fixed, all six now have a test, and
  writing those tests found a seventh the review had not reached: five of the
  six figures were drawn wider than the text column, figure 3 at more than twice
  it, so its lettering would have printed at under 4 pt (FIG-04, FIG-05, FIG-06,
  FIG-07, C3T-11, DEV-03).

- Setting matplotlib's `ps.fonttype` to 42, so that the EPS figures embed a font
  program as the venue requires, changed the **PDF** output as well and made
  figure 3 irreproducible. The rcParam names the PostScript backend and looked
  safe to set globally. **Caught by
  `tests/test_figures.py::test_regeneration_is_byte_identical`**, one run later.
  It is now set inside an `rc_context` scoped to the EPS writes.

## Accountability

The author is responsible for every claim in this repository and in any
manuscript drawn from it, including those produced with LLM assistance. The
controls above exist so that responsibility can be discharged by checking rather
than by trust.
